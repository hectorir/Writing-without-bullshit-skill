# Blog Post Playbook

Loaded when the format is blog post or article. Apply alongside the 10 line-edit habits in `SKILL.md`.

Target length: under 750 words. Longer posts lose readers; if you need more, you need a report or a series.

## ROAM target sentence

Before drafting:

> *After reading this, [current and potential readers] will [learn something useful], so they will [share the post / subscribe / take a specific action] and think of me as [a helpful expert on this topic].*

If you can't write this sentence, the post isn't ready.

## The hook does most of the work

The title and first sentences carry the post. They are also what shows up in:
- Google search results
- Social media link previews
- Email previews when shared

Spend disproportionate time on the title. Test: if a reader sees only the title, does it communicate something useful?

- ✅ "Three numbers that explain why your team's velocity dropped"
- ❌ "Some thoughts on velocity"

The first sentence should reward the click. Front-load the payoff.

## Structural toolkit

Walls of paragraphs lose readers, especially on phones. Use:

- **Subheads** as guideposts (max two levels)
- **Bullets with bolded openers** for skimmability
- **Numbered lists** when sequence or count matters
- **Tables** for parallel structured information
- **Graphics** — even simple Venn diagrams or flow charts carry meaning alongside text
- **Pull quotes** to highlight the one line you want shared
- **Links** to your sources and related posts

A reader who skims your subheads, bullets, and graphics should walk away with the main message. If they would not, restructure.

## Graphics matter

Include at least one shareable graphic with your URL embedded in it. When the post gets shared on social or screenshotted, the graphic carries the attribution.

Graphics work in parallel with text, not as an afterthought. Plan them while outlining; do not bolt them on at the end.

## Promotion

Writing the post is half the work. Plan the rest:

- Share on the social platforms where your audience reads
- Front-load every promotional post (see `social-and-press.md`)
- Respond to comments — engagement compounds
- Pitch to newsletters or aggregators that cover your topic

## SEO last

Genuinely useful content ranks. Keyword stuffing does not. Worry about SEO after the post is good — pick a target phrase, make sure the title and first paragraph use it once naturally, then move on.

## Common failure modes

- **Burying the lead** — the actual point shows up in paragraph six. Move it to the first sentence.
- **No call to action** — readers finish, like it, and bounce. Tell them what to do next: subscribe, share, read the related post, comment.
- **Generic title** — "Thoughts on X." Earn the title.
- **Hedging conclusions** — "It depends" is rarely the most useful answer. Take a position.
