# Blog Post Playbook

Loaded for: blog post, article. Apply alongside 10 habits in `SKILL.md`.

Target: <750 words. Longer → use report or series.

## ROAM target sentence

Before drafting:

> *After reading this, [current and potential readers] will [learn something useful], so they will [share the post / subscribe / take a specific action] and think of me as [a helpful expert on this topic].*

Can't write it → post not ready.

## The hook does most of the work

Title + first sentences carry post. They show up in:
- Google search results
- Social media link previews
- Email previews when shared

Spend disproportionate time on title. Reader sees only title → does it communicate something useful?

- ✅ "Three numbers that explain why your team's velocity dropped"
- ❌ "Some thoughts on velocity"

First sentence rewards click. Front-load payoff.

## Structural toolkit

Walls of paragraphs lose readers, esp. on phones. Use:

- **Subheads** as guideposts (max two levels)
- **Bullets with bolded openers** for skimmability
- **Numbered lists** when sequence or count matters
- **Tables** for parallel structured information
- **Graphics** — even simple Venn diagrams or flow charts carry meaning alongside text
- **Pull quotes** to highlight the one line you want shared
- **Links** to your sources and related posts

Reader skimming subheads, bullets, graphics → should get main message. If not → restructure.

## Graphics matter

Include ≥1 shareable graphic with URL embedded. Shared/screenshotted → graphic carries attribution.

Graphics work in parallel with text, not afterthought. Plan while outlining; don't bolt on at end.

## Promotion

Writing is half the work. Plan rest:

- Share on social where audience reads
- Front-load every promotional post (see `social-and-press.md`)
- Respond to comments — engagement compounds
- Pitch newsletters/aggregators covering your topic

## SEO last

Genuinely useful content ranks. Keyword stuffing does not. SEO after post is good — pick target phrase, use in title + first para once naturally, move on.

## Common failure modes

- **Burying lead** — point shows up in para 6. Move to first sentence.
- **No call to action** — readers finish + bounce. Tell them next: subscribe, share, read related post, comment.
- **Generic title** — "Thoughts on X." Earn it.
- **Hedging conclusions** — "It depends" rarely most useful. Take position.
