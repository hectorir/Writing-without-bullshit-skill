# Email Playbook

Loaded for: email, message, reply, "send to." Apply alongside 10 habits in `SKILL.md`.

Target: <250 words. Need more → use different format (memo, doc link, meeting).

## Anatomy of a good email

Six parts, in order. All required.

1. **Subject line carries the ask.** Put action + deadline.
   - ✅ "Need your sign-off on the contract by Thursday"
   - ❌ "Quick question"
   - ❌ "Following up"

2. **One-line greeting.** Recipient knows you → name only. If not → one line of context: "I'm a colleague of Alan's in finance."

3. **One-sentence summary.** Whole point, up front. Read nothing else → this sentence lands. Front-load aggressively.

4. **Facts on one topic.** One email = one topic. Bullets, **bolded openers**, links for detail. Two topics → two emails.

5. **Call to action with deadline.** What + by when. Specific date beats "ASAP."

6. **Brief sign-off.** Name + contact. No "Have a great day," no "Hope this finds you well," no "Don't hesitate to reach out."

## Replies

First line answers the question or gives the decision. Context after, if needed. Quote only the line you're answering.

- ✅ "Yes, Friday works. I'll send the deck Thursday."
- ❌ "Thanks for reaching out about this! I've had a look at the options you mentioned and…"

## Etiquette rules

- **Reply, not reply-all.** Smallest audience that needs it.
- **Nothing to add → send nothing.** "Got it" and "Thanks!" are noise. User asks for one → say so in one line, then draft if they still want it.
- **Read latest thread message first.** Problem may already be solved.
- **After 3 back-and-forths, call.** Email failing if it takes 4 rounds.

## Cold email template

Asking stranger for help: customize each. <150 words.

Required elements:

1. **Show you know who they are.** One specific ref — post, project, public comment. User supplied none → `[specific episode/post you've actually read]` placeholder. Generic flattery ("I love your work") backfires.
2. **State ask plainly.** No five-para windup.
3. **What's in it for them.** Why interesting to *them*, not just you? One sentence.
4. **One clear next step.** Specific question, 15-min call w/ 2 time options, or yes/no. One, not a menu.

Avoid:
- Mass-blast templates with merge tags
- Long self-introductions
- Bulleted list of everything you could talk about
- Asks requiring significant recipient work before they know if they care
- Closing offers ("happy to send more detail")

## When you're delivering bad news

Lead with problem. Take responsibility. List actions as bullets. End with what's next or relevant context. Cheerful sign-offs after bad news make things worse.
